COMPOSITION

A dark, cinematic photographic scene fills the frame — the kind of staged
photograph a tabloid would run to imply that something enormous is happening.
Real lighting, real depth of field, real materials. The scene should depict the
literal, surface reading of the headline, not its actual subject: whatever the
headline says is happening, show exactly that, played completely seriously.

Typeset the headline into the upper portion of the frame in a heavy condensed
sans-serif, tight leading, white on the dark background. Pull out the single
most sensational word or number and set it far larger, in a hand-painted yellow
brush script, as though someone circled it. Set the subheading smaller, in a
casual handwritten marker face, off to one side.

Scatter a few small supporting details through the scene the way a busy front
page does: a handwritten sticky note, a monitor readout with a couple of lines
of monospaced status text, a boxed warning label with a triangle icon, an arrow
drawn in marker pointing at something. Keep these short — two or three words
each. They should read as marginalia, not as paragraphs.

TYPOGRAPHY MUST BE CORRECT

Every word rendered in the image must be spelled exactly as given above.
Reproduce the headline text verbatim, character for character. Do not invent
additional sentences, captions, bylines, datelines, page numbers, prices,
barcodes or masthead text. If a piece of text will not render cleanly, leave it
out rather than rendering it garbled.

HARD CONSTRAINTS

- No real, recognisable or identifiable people. Figures may appear, but from
  behind, in silhouette, at a distance, or cropped so no face is legible.
- No logos, wordmarks, brand names, institutional crests, university seals,
  journal names, publisher marks or newspaper nameplates of any kind. No text
  that names a real organisation.
- No charts, graphs, plots, tables or equations that could be mistaken for
  genuine scientific results. Readouts may show status words, never data.
- No signatures, artist marks, watermarks, stock-photo overlays or borders.
- No gore, no nudity, no children, no depictions of real recent events, and
  nothing that could read as a genuine news photograph of an actual incident.

Output one image. No frame, no mat, no surrounding page — the artwork fills the
canvas edge to edge.
